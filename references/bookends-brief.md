# Bookends brief (overview and closing chapters)

You write `chapters/00-overview.md` and `chapters/99-closing.md`. Your inputs are the output of
`fg.py digest` (every finished chapter's recap and open gaps), `plan.json`, `map.md` and
`field-guide.config.json`. **Don't open source code or the chapters themselves.** The digest is enough, and
keeping it that way is what keeps this step cheap on huge codebases. Read the **Voice** section of
`references/writer.md` for voice (use `config.tone`) and callout labels.

Keep turns to a minimum, since each tool call re-sends your context: read `plan.json`, `map.md`'s
first 40 lines and `writer.md` in one parallel turn, then write both files in one parallel turn,
then reply. No re-reading after writing.

## 00-overview.md (about 500-900 words; start at `##`, the build adds the title)

- `## What this system is`: a few plain sentences on what it does and for whom, then the one promise or
  central tension everything else is built around, if the digest shows one (e.g. "never corrupt the
  user's file", "tenant data must never leak").
- `## The shape of it`: one Mermaid diagram of the major parts and how a request or action flows
  between them. Only boxes the chapters actually explain.
- `## How to read this guide`: the chapter list grouped as foundations, then concepts. One line per
  chapter on the question it answers. Mention that each chapter declares its reading direction.
  If some planned chapters aren't written yet, say so plainly.
- If `plan.json` has `not_covered`, one short paragraph naming those areas and why they're left out.

## 99-closing.md

- `## What's still open`: **at most 10 items**, one plain line each, tagged with the chapter that
  explains it ("(chapter 5)"). Pick `[high]` gaps first, then the `[med]` ones that most change how a
  reader should think about the system. Merge related gaps into one line. Doc drift and stale comments
  get at most one line in total. End with one sentence pointing to `FINDINGS.md`, the full cited list
  for the team, generated by `fg.py build`. This section is a map of the sharp edges, not a bug tracker.
- `## The whole thing, out loud`: two to four paragraphs that tie the chapter recaps into one story.
  Follow one action through the whole system if possible. This is the recap of recaps; no new facts.

The glossary is appended automatically by `fg.py build`. Don't write one.

Reply in under 80 words: both paths and word counts.
